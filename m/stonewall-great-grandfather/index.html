Why This Page Exists

His stone in Stonewall says a name and two dates, and nothing else. No story. No work, no loves, no trace of the life between those numbers.

His great-grandson stood at that stone and wanted to know who he was — and the stone could not answer. This page is the family's answer. Everything we know lives here. What we don't know yet is written down honestly, as an open door: anyone who remembers him, in any way, is invited to help us finish it.

A name and two dates are where a person is buried. A story is where he lives.

What We Know

  • He rests in Stonewall, Oklahoma, under a stone that carries only his name and his birth and death dates.
  • He is the great-grandfather of Kenny Rogers of Cache, Oklahoma — and of Kenny's brothers, Sonny and Anthony.
  • He was born into a family that had already survived more than most families are ever asked to survive — and kept going. That endurance is part of his story, and it is told below the way his family tells it.

The Line He Comes From

As the family tells it: generations before him, an enslaved man ran from beatings and mistreatment and found refuge among Choctaw people in Pearl, Mississippi. When the Choctaw were forced west, the family walked the Trail of Tears from Mississippi to Durant, Oklahoma. In time, the family received land through the Sooner Land Run and put down roots in Oklahoma earth.

Our great-grandfather inherited all of that — the escape, the refuge, the walk, the land — the way you inherit a heartbeat. Whatever his daily life held, he carried a line that refused to end. It hasn't. It runs through his great-grandson today: Kenny Rogers is an enrolled citizen of the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma, a member by blood through his Mississippi Choctaw lineage.

Both Lineages, One Page

His blood carries two peoples: an African line that survived slavery, and a Choctaw line that survived removal. On paper, enrollment asks a family to choose one line and let the other go quiet.

A family's memory doesn't have to choose. This page keeps both — names, stories, and heritage from every branch, side by side, the way they actually lived in him.

In the full service, this page can carry family trees for both lineages, and tribal-language words and audio — developed with speakers of the language, never guessed at.
